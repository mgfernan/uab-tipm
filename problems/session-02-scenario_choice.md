# Session 2: Orbit and beam strategy scenario choice

## Purpose

Practice choosing an orbit and beam strategy from service requirements, then defending the trade-offs. There is no single correct answer: a good recommendation connects the scenario's priorities to coverage, capacity, latency, mobility, and continuity.

## Activity

Work in pairs or small groups. Select one scenario from the list below. Then, follow these steps:

1. Choose an orbit: **GEO, MEO, or LEO**.
2. Choose a beam strategy: **broad beam, several smaller spot beams, or moving/steered beams**.
3. Identify the main trade-off your choice accepts (i.e. what can be sacrificed to meet the most important requirement?).
4. Change one of the requirements to see how your choice would change. For example, if you initially prioritized continuity, what would you choose if you instead prioritized capacity?
5. Prepare a short justification using this prompt:

   > We chose ___ orbit and ___ beams because the most important requirement is ___. We accept ___ as the trade-off. If instead we prioritized ___, we would choose ___.

## Scenarios

### 1. Remote schools

Connect a few schools across a large, low-density region. Lessons are mostly pre-recorded or asynchronous, and service continuity matters more than interactive response time.

### 2. Busy shipping lane

Provide broadband along a heavily traveled sea corridor. Ships move continuously, and demand is concentrated along the route.

### 3. Regional disaster communications

Keep basic voice, messaging, and low-rate coordination available across a large region for several weeks. Response teams are spread out, and predictable continuous coverage matters more than broadband speed.

### 4. Rural broadband hotspot

Serve a small town with poor terrestrial broadband. Demand peaks in the evening, and users are concentrated in the town center.

### 5. Polar research stations

Connect a few stations in a remote polar region. They exchange data and need dependable access, but do not require high interactive speeds.

### 6. Connected aircraft

Offer passenger internet on aircraft crossing oceans. Aircraft move quickly, and many passengers share the connection at once.

### 7. Environmental sensors

Connect low-rate sensors distributed across a large forest. Devices send small reports periodically; long battery life matters more than speed.

### 8. Major event

Provide temporary extra capacity for a festival in a remote area. Demand is very high for a few days and then disappears.

## Debrief

- Which requirement most influenced your choice: coverage, capacity, latency, mobility, or continuity?
- What changed when you prioritized a different requirement?
