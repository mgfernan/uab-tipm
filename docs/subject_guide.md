## Prerequisites

Students are recommended to have prior knowledge of:

- Fundamentals of telecommunication systems and networks.  
- Digital transmission and mobile communications.  
- Basic concepts of radio propagation and spectrum usage.  
- Elementary notions of network dimensioning and traffic analysis.  
- Basic use of calculation, simulation, or programming tools for technical scenario analysis.  

## Objectives

Planning and managing telecommunication infrastructures requires linking service demand with technical design decisions, regulatory constraints, and economic feasibility criteria. This course provides an integrated view of network deployment, from requirement definition to decision-making on coverage, capacity, transport, costs, energy efficiency, and network evolution strategy.

The subject is approached from an applied perspective, aiming to enable students to translate real deployment scenarios into solutions justified from both engineering and business viewpoints. Throughout the course, a comparative dimension between terrestrial networks and satellite solutions will also be introduced.

The course objectives are:

- Interpret service needs and translate them into quantifiable technical requirements.  
- Establish demand models and define key network performance indicators.  
- Dimension a radio access layer consistent with a given demand and justify trade-offs between coverage and capacity.  
- Design the transport network associated with access, considering traffic aggregation, latency, synchronization, and reliability.  
- Evaluate the economic feasibility of a deployment using simplified models of CAPEX (capital expenditure), OPEX (operational expenditure), and ROI (return on investment).  
- Integrate energy efficiency and sustainability criteria into design decisions.  
- Analyze the impact of regulatory frameworks and spectrum on telecommunications infrastructure design.  
- Develop phased deployment strategies and defend technical and economic decisions in realistic environments.  
- Relate network planning concepts to real-world cases.  

## Contents

### 1. From Demand to Design

- Interpretation of service needs.  
- Translation of service needs into quantifiable technical requirements.  
- Definition of good and poor network requirements.  
- Demand models.  
- Use scenarios: dense urban, rural, industrial, and mobility corridors.  
- Estimation of traffic demand, user density, and quality-of-service requirements.  
- Network KPIs: capacity per unit area, target latency, and availability.  
- Identification of typical traffic profiles.  

### 2. Radio Dimensioning

- From traffic requirements to radio infrastructure.  
- Coverage, capacity, and spectrum usage.  
- Simplified link budget.  
- Spectral efficiency.  
- Differences between sub-6 GHz and mmWave bands.  
- Cell size definition.  
- Dimensioning the number of radio nodes and required spectrum.  
- Trade-offs between coverage and capacity.  
- Impact of densification on overall performance.  

### 3. Network Transport

- Transmission technologies: optical fiber and radio links.  
- Backhaul dimensioning and transport architectures.  
- Network topologies: star, ring, and mesh.  
- Traffic aggregation and oversubscription.  
- Latency, synchronization, and reliability.  
- Design of a transport network adapted to a specific scenario.  

### 4. Economic Aspects

- Cost model and feasibility.  
- CAPEX: equipment, infrastructure, and spectrum.  
- OPEX: energy, maintenance, and leasing.  
- Economic KPIs: cost per user and cost per Mbps.  
- Network cost models.  
- ROI, payback period, and break-even.  

### 5. Energy Efficiency

- Energy impact of telecommunication networks.  
- Relationship between consumption, operational cost, and sustainability.  
- Quantification of radio node energy consumption.  
- Impact of traffic and frequency band on consumption.  
- Optimization and energy-saving strategies.  
- Energy KPIs and impact on OPEX.  

### 6. Regulation and Spectrum

- Regulatory constraints and opportunities in deployment.  
- Spectrum allocation: auctions and licenses.  
- Characteristics of different frequency bands.  
- Emission limits, exposure constraints, and coverage obligations.  
- Infrastructure and spectrum sharing.  

### 7. Deployment Strategies

- Decision-making in real-world environments.  
- Deployment phases: from coverage to densification and capacity.  
- Scenario-dependent strategies.  
- Progressive deployment and sharing.  
- Technical versus economic decisions.  
- Analysis of real operator cases.  

Each of these sessions will be applied to both terrestrial and satellite network cases.

## Methodology

Learning will combine theoretical lectures, case analysis, and practical sessions focused on network design and evaluation. The course follows an applied approach, where concepts introduced in lectures are later developed through realistic deployment scenarios and reference cases.

Three types of activities are defined:

### Autonomous Activities

- Individual study of theoretical foundations and recommended bibliography.  
- Preparation of exercises, scenario analysis, and problem-solving in planning, dimensioning, and feasibility.  
- Progressive development of the integrative project carried out during practical sessions.  

### Guided Activities

- In-person theoretical lectures introducing models, tools, and decision criteria for telecommunication infrastructure design.  
- Practical sessions focused on applying course concepts to specific network scenarios.  
- In each main block, part of the teaching will map theoretical concepts to real cases, linking theory, engineering, and industrial reality.  

### Supervised Activities

- Tutorials and monitoring of the integrative project.  
- Supervision during practical sessions and guidance in technical and economic decision-making.  

Practical sessions are organized into four milestones of an integrative project. Students will define a scenario, dimension the access solution, propose a transport network, incorporate economic, energy, and regulatory criteria, and prepare a final defense.

## Assessment

The course includes three assessment components:

- ET: Terrestrial exam (25%).  
- ES: Satellite exam (25%).  
- PROJ: Deployment project and final defense (50%).  

The integrative project (PROJ) consists of designing a complete network for a specific deployment scenario. Students must justify decisions from both technical and business perspectives. The project is developed across four practical sessions and concludes with a final oral presentation.

Project assessment includes checkpoints after each session and a final presentation:

- CP1: Scenario definition, assumptions, and requirements (10% of final grade).  
- CP2: Access layer design and preliminary dimensioning (10%).  
- CP3: Transport network, economic model, and feasibility (10%).  
- CP4: Final integration, regulation, energy, and deployment strategy (10%).  
- PF: Oral presentation and defense (60%).  

Final grade calculation:

- If \( 	ext{ET} \geq 3.5 \) and \( 	ext{ES} \geq 3.5 \):  
  \( 	ext{Final Grade} = 0.25 \cdot 	ext{ET} + 0.25 \cdot 	ext{ES} + 0.50 \cdot 	ext{PROJ} \)

- If \( 	ext{ET} < 3.5 \) or \( 	ext{ES} < 3.5 \):  
  \( 	ext{Final Grade} = \min(	ext{ET}, 	ext{ES}) \)

### Retake Policy

To pass the course, \( 	ext{Final Grade} \geq 5 \). Students below this threshold may take a retake exam covering the exam components. The integrative project is not recoverable due to its continuous and applied nature.

### “Not Assessable” Status

Students who do not attend any written exams or participate in minimum continuous assessment activities will receive a “Not Assessable” status.

### Additional Considerations

Academic misconduct (e.g., copying or allowing copying of assessed work, including reports, calculations, documents, or presentations) will result in a failing grade for the activity and may lead to failing the course.

Linguistic correctness and clarity of argumentation may also be considered in grading reports, submissions, and presentations.

This course does not include a single final assessment option.

## Course Planning

Practical sessions may be carried out using spreadsheets, Python notebooks, MATLAB, or equivalent open-source tools, depending on the exercise type and design scenario.

### Use of AI

Teaching materials will be structured to be reusable in AI-assisted study environments (e.g., NotebookLM) and documentation platforms.

The use of artificial intelligence (AI) technologies is allowed as part of coursework, provided that the final result reflects a significant student contribution in analysis and personal reflection. Students must clearly identify which parts were generated using AI. Lack of transparency in AI use is considered academic dishonesty and may result in grade penalties or more severe sanctions in serious cases.
